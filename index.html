Reading risk before it becomes an emergency

This site works through a dataset of 1014 pregnancies monitored across hospitals, community clinics, and maternal health cares using low-cost IoT risk sensors. Six signals — age, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, blood glucose, body temperature, and heart rate — are used to assign each pregnancy a clinician-labeled risk level: low, mid, or high. The goal here is the same one behind most early-warning systems in maternal health: figure out which combinations of ordinary vitals are already telling us something, well before an outcome forces the question.

What’s in the data

Each row is one pregnancy, described by six variables collected through IoT-based patient monitoring at the point of care, plus a risk level assigned from those readings.

Variable Description Units
age Age of the woman at the time of pregnancy years
systolic_bp Upper value of blood pressure mmHg
diastolic_bp Lower value of blood pressure mmHg
bs Blood glucose level mmol/L
body_temp Body temperature °F
heart_rate Resting heart rate bpm
risk_level Clinician-assigned risk during pregnancy low / mid / high

Source: Maternal Health Risk Data Set, Kaggle. Collected from hospitals, community clinics, and maternal health cares via IoT-based risk monitoring.
